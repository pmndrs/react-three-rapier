Rapier is fast, but every body, collider and contact costs time on every step. Most performance problems come from
the number of colliders and their complexity.

## Prefer simple colliders

- `cuboid` and `ball` are the cheapest shapes. Use them whenever the visual shape is close enough.
- `hull` is a good compromise for convex objects. It is much cheaper than `trimesh`.
- `trimesh` is only appropriate for fixed bodies (level geometry). Dynamic trimesh bodies are expensive and unreliable.
- Hand-place a few simple colliders on a [compound body](/api/colliders#collider-components) instead of one detailed mesh collider.
- Turn off automatic colliders you do not need: `<Physics colliders={false}>`.

## Use instancing for many identical objects

Use [`InstancedRigidBodies`](/api/instanced-rigid-bodies) rather than hundreds of separate meshes. Each instance still
costs a rigid body in the simulation, but you pay only one draw call. Give every instance a stable `key`.

## Let bodies sleep

Bodies at rest are put to sleep and skipped by the solver. Rapier 0.21 sleeps bodies sooner than 0.19 did. Do not set
`canSleep={false}` unless you need to. Sleeping is also what lets [on-demand rendering](/advanced/update-loop#on-demand-rendering)
idle.

## Tune the solver sparingly

`numSolverIterations`, `numInternalPgsIterations` and `additionalSolverIterations` trade stability for speed. Raise them
only for the bodies or scenes that need it. Use `ccd` on small, fast bodies rather than very small time steps.

## Reduce overhead

- Leave `debug` off in production.
- Avoid running heavy code in `onCollisionEnter` and `onContactForce`, which fire often.
- Do not create many `<Physics>` worlds.
